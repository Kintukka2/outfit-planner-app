# Whering and Alta Lead 2026's Closet Apps

The best outfit-planner and digital-wardrobe apps on the App Store in late October 2026 are **Whering, Alta, Acloset and Indyx**, with **Stylebook** as the paid veteran. A reproducible composite of store ratings, international reach, charts, update recency, editorial coverage and Reddit recommendations scores them **87.9, 78.9, 73.4, 67.8 and 57.4**, and the next tier (Combyne, Cladwell, Pureple, Fits, Style DNA, GetWardrobe, Essembl) is a statistical tie. Each leader does a different job. Whering is the best free all-rounder, with an uncapped closet, a *Clueless*-style outfit shuffle, styling of friends' closets, no ads and AI sold by the credit. Alta is the fastest-growing and most technically ambitious (17,389 US ratings at 4.89 in 19 months, generative capture, roughly 20-second layered avatar try-on, $31M raised) but draws avatar-quality complaints and a Reddit astroturfing allegation. Acloset has the deepest import and AI tooling but caps free use at 100 items, Indyx pairs a free unlimited closet with paid analytics and human stylists, and Stylebook remains the private, one-time-purchase iOS power tool, unchanged since June 2025. Under the hood most closed apps buy the same pipeline of background removal, vision-model tagging, LLM outfit generation over a structured closet plus weather and calendar, and Gemini-class try-on, so competition has shifted to how little effort capture takes, how planning is modelled and how pricing is designed. Users reward the plain utility loop of planning, packing, wearing more and buying less far more than AI, and they punish paywalls that land after hours of cataloguing: monetisation appears in **53% of 1★ reviews**, and **90% of reviews that mention billing problems are 1★**. Commercially, no pure closet subscription has reached venture scale (Cladwell sold at about $330K ARR), so capital is flowing to B2B styling infrastructure, commission-earning shopping agents and resale or data plays, just as Google Photos' Wardrobe and ChatGPT's try-on begin giving away the generic features standalone apps used to sell.

### Critics agree on a canon, while Reddit users doubt Alta

Independent 2025–26 "best of" lists are scarce, and most ranking content is SEO copy by rival apps that copy one another's shortlists. Across 18 competitor-authored roundups (excluding each author's own app), Acloset appears in 17, Whering and Stylebook in 15, Indyx in 13, Cladwell in 12, and Alta and Pureple in 9 each. Their stock verdicts are that Whering is "best free", Indyx is best for analytics and human stylists, Stylebook is the manual one-time-purchase tool, Acloset is best for automated AI cataloguing (with a 100-item free cap), Cladwell is the capsule app and Alta is the free AI stylist with avatars ([Wardrowbe roundup](https://wardrowbe.com/blog/best-wardrobe-apps-2026/); [Stylin comparison](https://stylinai.com/compare/best-virtual-wardrobe-app.html)). Alta gets by far the most independent press. TechCrunch covered its seed round and its brand partnerships, ELLE called it "the smart closet we were promised in Clueless", and Vogue Business covered its e.l.f. Beauty deal. A press release says TIME named it a Best Inventions 2025 special mention, though TIME's own page could not be checked ([ELLE](https://www.elle.com/fashion/personal-style/a65551355/alta-ai-closet-styling-app-announcement/); [Vogue Business](https://www.vogue.com/article/why-elf-beauty-is-building-avatars-with-ai-platform-alta); [ABNewswire via FinancialContent](https://markets.financialcontent.com/pennwell.elp/article/abnewswire-2025-10-22-time-magazine-and-fast-company-have-selected-ai-styling-app-alta-for-their-lists-of-best-inventions-and-next-big-things-in-tech-of-2025)).

Reddit tells a more sceptical story. Across 29 recommendation threads from June 2024 to August 2026 (about 828 posts and comments), people named Whering in 20 threads, Indyx in 17, Acloset in 16, Alta in 14, Stylebook in 13 and OpenWardrobe in 11, while spreadsheets, Notion and Pinterest recurred as the real substitutes. Power users in r/capsulewardrobe and r/femalefashionadvice value one-time pricing, cost-per-wear stats and data ownership over generated outfits. One praised Stylebook as a "single cost to download and doesn't use a subscription model" ([r/femalefashionadvice, March 2026](https://www.reddit.com/r/femalefashionadvice/comments/1s242ng/digital_wardrobe_apps/)). In the same thread a commenter alleged that threads are "cluttered with heaps of bot comments praising Alta… posted way later than the original thread". Comment timing fits the charge: **71% of Alta-mentioning comments (75 of 105) arrived more than seven days after the thread opened**, against 15% for Indyx, 29% for Whering, 32% for Stylebook and 41% for Acloset. That pattern is consistent with astroturfing but does not prove it.

### Whering runs a free, uncapped closet as a social styling game

Whering is a London company founded by former banker Bianca Rangecroft. It was incorporated in 2020, and press dates the launch to 2021. It bills itself as "the world's largest free social styling & closet app" and claims **9M+ users on the App Store and 10M+ on its website**, mostly Gen Z, with a team of 19. Its **$7M seed in July 2026**, led by eBay Ventures and the Google AI Futures Fund, is earmarked for image enhancement, gallery scanning, virtual try-on and resale ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1519461680); [whering.co](https://whering.co/); [Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/07/07/whering-lands-7m-as-digital-wardrobe-platform-reaches-10m-users/); [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/news/whering-seed-funding-ai/)).

Capture leans on the crowd. Users add items from the camera roll or from a **"database of over 100 million items"** uploaded by other users. They can also pull pieces from friends' closets, use the share sheet or in-app Google search, or install a Chrome extension (about 10,000 users) that puts a save button on any product image. Background removal is automatic but cannot be edited, photos cannot be rotated, and each item gets a single photo. The privacy policy names remove.bg (Kaleido AI) and the fashion-tagging vendor Pixyle as processors, and an Enhance feature added in April 2026 turns rough snaps into product-style images ([Whering FAQ](https://whering.co.uk/faq); [Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/whering/kjdgcemdlnbdohbcmnamlnnfkjoijfol); [Whering privacy policy](https://whering.co/privacy-policy)). Items carry editable auto-tags plus private tags, brand, price, seasons, occasions and a "Status" tag for states such as "in the wash". The constraints are noticeable: there is only one wardrobe, sizes are UK-only, and items display in upload order. Wishlists (in folders), moodboards and lookbooks sit around the edges ([FAQ](https://whering.co.uk/faq)).

Outfit-making has three layers. A free-form Canvas lets users drag, resize and layer pieces. **Dress Me**, the "Clueless-inspired" slot machine, shuffles category rows in 2-, 3- or 4-piece layouts and lets users pin pieces. It filters by season, colour, favourites or least-worn items and can exclude activewear or underwear. AI suggestions arrive two ways. "W Pick", a long-running beta, serves **six outfits a day** from owned items, weather and past choices, and a 2026 Planner suggests outfits from wardrobe plus forecast and lets users change location to plan trips ([FAQ](https://whering.co.uk/faq); [Style Within Grace review](https://stylewithingrace.com/whering-wardrobe-app-review/); [App Store version history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1519461680)). Events record dates, location and mood and can generate a packing list from each day's outfits, prompting "Have you thought about adding these?". An outfit-selfie tab (December 2025) handles wear logging, and a Challenges Hub runs a 60-day styling challenge ([Challenges Hub](https://whering.co.uk/thoughts/challenges-hub-launched)).

Analytics, which Whering pitches as "Spotify Wrapped but for your wardrobe", cover wardrobe value, the share of items worn, cost per wear, new-versus-preloved intake, a colour-palette "gaps" view and "closet longevity", and a seasonal **Unpacked** recap has run since April 2026 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1519461680)). The Community tab carries For You, Following, Outfit Selfies and Style Submissions feeds. **"Style [username]" lets anyone build an outfit for another user from that user's own clothes**, which is the category's signature social mechanic ([FAQ](https://whering.co.uk/faq)). Commerce stays light. Wishlists link out to retailers, and the app points to repair, laundry and resale partners (Sojo, The Seam, Oxwash, Kair, Thrift+). The September 2026 terms describe eBay listing, sponsored "Brand Wardrobes" and a third-party-AI "W-Chat" assistant, none of which could be independently confirmed as live ([Whering services](https://whering.co.uk/services); [Whering terms](https://whering.co/terms-conditions)).

Pricing is unusual for the category. "Whering doesn't have a subscription… Access runs on a credit system": all standard features are free, the company says it shows **no ads** or sponsored clothes, and only Enhance, Virtual Try-On and Gallery Scanning cost credits. Every try-on **and every regeneration** costs one credit, and gallery scanning costs one credit per item digitised. The US store sells 10 credits for $2.99 and 100 for $12.99, while the help centre quotes lower British prices (100 for £6.99) ([Whering help, "Is Whering free?"](https://whering.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/5473162627231); [Whering help, try-on cost](https://whering.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/5800046039327-How-much-does-each-Virtual-Try-On-cost); [Whering help, top-up plans](https://whering.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/5800092986143-What-are-the-top-up-plans-for-Virtual-Try-On-credits)). The terms allow model training on uploads with an opt-out, and users cannot export their data ([terms §10](https://whering.co/terms-conditions); [FAQ](https://whering.co.uk/faq)). The interface uses an acid-lime, cyan and magenta Gen-Z palette over a five-tab bar of Community, Shop, Planner, Styling and Wardrobe.

### Alta builds the generative Clueless closet and sells it to brands

Alta Daily comes from Flagship AI, Inc. in New York. Founder and CEO Jenny Wang is a Harvard-trained engineer and former investor who still codes daily. The app reached the App Store on 13 March 2025, pitched as "your personal AI stylist… built around the clothes you already own… free to use". Alta raised an **$11M seed led by Menlo Ventures** in June 2025, with backers including the Arnault family's Aglaé, Anthropic's Anthology Fund and Karlie Kloss. A **$20M Series A, pre-empted by Forerunner, followed on 22 October 2026**. At that raise Alta reported **700M outfits and 20M avatars generated**, 12 in-house AI models and a 10-person team ([TechCrunch, June 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/16/alta-raises-11m-to-bring-clueless-fashion-tech-to-life-with-all-star-investors/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/alta-ai-fashion-app-series-a-20-million-forerunner-rent-the-runway/)).

Capture is Alta's deepest moat. Users can forward email receipts, pick pieces from Alta's product database, or photograph an item and let Alta find the original product photo. **Prettify** removes backgrounds and smooths wrinkles and shadows into e-commerce-style images, and a mirror selfie can be split into individual Prettified pieces. **Closet Scan**, launched on 25 October 2026, is a live-video mode: users prop the phone up and hold items to the front camera, and Alta claims about **100 items in five minutes**. Since November 2025 users have also been able to book a TaskRabbit Tasker to photograph their closet ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6481705400); [Alta blog, Prettify](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/prettify); [Alta blog, Closet Scan](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/closet-scanner); [Alta blog, TaskRabbit](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/taskrabbit)).

Generation runs on context. The Home screen offers daily outfits for the user's closet, weather and schedule. A read-only Google Calendar connection reads event titles, times and locations; a provider called TypeSafe classifies the events, and **Google Cloud Vertex AI generates event outfits**. A prompt box ("ask Alta anything") accepts briefs such as "festival outfit with converse", users can build a look around one item, and ratings, saves and dismissals train a per-account model ([Alta privacy policy](https://www.altadaily.com/privacy); [Alta blog, features](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/alta-main-features)). Avatars take a headshot, an "About You" form (height, measurements, skin tone) and an optional full-body selfie. Alta claims **about 20 seconds per render and up to eight layered items**, against Zara's four items in over two minutes, though Alta published that comparison itself ([Alta blog, avatars](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/virtual-dressing-room); [Alta blog, Alta vs Zara](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/alta-vs-zara)). Around this sit a calendar log with streaks, trip planning that turns cities, activities and luggage size into a packing list and lookbook, most- and least-worn items with cost per wear, and a June 2026 community in which any look can be tried on the viewer's own avatar, a weekly Top 100 ranks looks by views, likes and try-ons, and friends can restyle one another's outfits ([Alta blog, trip planning](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/trip-planning); [Alta blog, community](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/community)).

Commerce and B2B carry the business. Consumers see gap-filling shopping suggestions, sale alerts and Poshmark secondhand bags, and the founder told WWD that "if someone buys something, Alta gets a cut" ([WWD](https://wwd.com/fashion-news/fashion-features/cfda-partnership-alta-personal-styling-app-ai-1237089028/)). Brands use Alta as infrastructure. Public School added a "Style with Alta" button to its product pages, **Alta "now powers all AI within the Rent the Runway app", which saw a 35% engagement lift**, Zimmermann put its SS27 runway collection into Alta within minutes of the Paris show and used try-on data to gauge production quantities, Gap is pursuing an integration, and e.l.f. Beauty signed a year-long exclusive beauty integration ([TechCrunch, February 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/14/clueless-inspired-app-alta-partners-with-brand-public-school-to-start-integrating-styling-tools-into-websites/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/alta-ai-fashion-app-series-a-20-million-forerunner-rent-the-runway/); [Alta blog, Zimmermann](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/zimmermann); [Vogue Business](https://www.vogue.com/article/why-elf-beauty-is-building-avatars-with-ai-platform-alta)).

The weaknesses come from the generative core. An October 2026 review says avatar quality "dropped so much". Another says Prettify "made up half my wardrobe turned my skirt into a shirt changed things color", which is the risk of generative clean-up compared with plain segmentation. A three-month daily user called the suggestions "occasionally brilliant, sometimes atrocious" and said avatar fit did not match real life ([Alta review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6481705400/sortby=mostrecent/json); [Technocouture](https://technocouture.substack.com/p/reflections-from-my-clueless-closet)).

### Acloset automates capture and styling, then meters both

Acloset is made by Looko Inc. in Seoul (CEO Go Haesin). It launched in February 2021 and ships in 18 languages. It claims **8M+ users and 110M+ digitised garments**, and a hiring post adds more than 1M new garments a week, break-even "last September" and revenue up 70–120% year on year. Its funding includes a KRW 3bn Series A (2022) led by KT Investment ([Acloset About](https://www.acloset.app/about/); [Wanted job post](https://www.wanted.co.kr/wd/391802); [VentureSquare](https://www.venturesquare.net/854274/)).

Acloset has the category's most complete "zero-photo" funnel for adding clothes without photographing them, and most of its 2026 releases touched import. Guests can add clothes before signing up, and the camera has single and continuous modes. **Smart Detector** splits a mirror selfie into up to four items. Users can also log in to a store such as Amazon, SHEIN, ZARA or ASOS inside an in-app browser and import their order records; passwords are not stored, and address and payment data are excluded. A read-only Gmail connection scans only receipts, deletes its scan history after seven days and saves nothing without the user's selection. A personal `@acloset.app` address accepts forwarded receipts. A Clothes Library searchable by text or image, product-code and tag-photo lookup, and a Chrome extension complete the set. **Beautify Pro**, which turns snapshots into store-style images, runs on **Gemini 2.5 Flash Image** ([Acloset FAQ](https://www.acloset.app/support/); [Beautify Pro announcement](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/meet-the-new-beautify-pro-39a609/); [6.21.0 notes](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/6-21-0-1776924729783/)). Acloset's attribute schema is the deepest in the category because its stylist consumes it. It covers category, colour, pattern, material, neck style, length, fit, season, occasion and laundry care, and a "Quick Closet Review" screen and a "Not set" filter keep those attributes clean. Multiple user-defined closets can share items ([5.24.0](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/5-24-0-update-news-623ca1/); [Closets announcement](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/now-you-can-organize-your-clothes-by-closet-the-way-you-want-e17003/)).

The AI layer recommends outfits by situation (Daily, School, Date, Wedding, Travel) or free text, using location, time, weekday and a forecast up to seven days out. **"Style with Items" completion** keeps the pieces a user has chosen and fills only the missing categories, never pairing incompatibles such as trousers with a skirt. Generated outfits auto-delete after 90 days. A chat stylist comes in four personas (Leo, Suzy, Jax and Clara) and also runs Find My Color (face photo to palette), Find My Fit (body photo to silhouettes) and Rate Style. Since July 2026 users can create an avatar from one full-body photo. Try-on is split into a **Basic tier with daily free uses and a Pro tier**, with a "Try a better version" option that reruns a result at Pro quality ([6.30.0](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/6-30-0-1785377094985/); [6.4.0](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/6-4-0-update-news-7a3c50/); [6.34.0](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/6-34-0-1789025177704/)). Planning covers an OOTD calendar, packing lists with destination weather, and home-screen widgets added in October 2026. **The suggestion widget switches to tomorrow's outfit at 6pm**, the most habit-forming planning mechanic in the set ([6.41.1](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/6-41-1-1791974915033/)). Community features relaunched in October 2026, but past commerce attempts were dropped: the Shop tab closed in 2022, and a Preloved resale market ran only from January 2023 to August 2024 ([Preloved closure](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/notice-of-secondhand-market-service-discontinuation-and-upco-8c4e21/)).

Pricing is built on item caps, ads since January 2024, and a currency called "Beans" (100 for $1.99) that pays for generative features. **Archived items count against the cap**, and there is no data export ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1542311809); [subscription overview](https://www.acloset.app/announcements/subscription-plans-overview-eb9fc8/)).

### Indyx keeps the closet free and the styling human

Indyx is a San Francisco startup led by Yidi Campbell, who previously worked in strategy at Gap and Athleta. It claims "tens of thousands of real digital closets and over 10 million digitized items". Its stance is explicit: **"Always a trusted style advisor, never a robot"**, and it has no algorithmic outfit generator ([Indyx home](https://www.myindyx.com/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1599179405)). A Style Quiz turns outfit photos into style words and archetypes that drive "Best Match" stylist matching. Every new user gets a **14-day Insider Preview** of all premium features before dropping to a generous free tier ([Indyx support FAQ](https://www.myindyx.com/contact-us)).

Capture combines automatic background removal and AI tags for category, subcategory and colour with two image tools. Enhance (May 2026) produces "editorial flatlays", and Refine (September 2026) lets users edit those flatlays with a text prompt. Purchases arrive through receipts forwarded to a personal `@scan.myindyx.com` address, a Gmail link that reads only Purchases-category mail, or a pasted URL. For people who will not catalogue at all, **"The Catalog" sends in-home Archivists who digitise 100 items in five hours** in select US metro areas, for about $295 plus $2 per additional item ([Indyx privacy policy](https://www.myindyx.com/privacy-policy); [Indyx Catalog blog](https://www.myindyx.com/blog/what-to-expect-during-a-pro-indyx-wardrobe-cataloging-session); [Indyx Catalog listing, indexed](https://myindyx.com/personal-stylists/pigmami/p/the-catalog)). Styling is a marketplace in which each stylist sets prices. Stylists need at least 25 indexed items and can see the whole wardrobe except items tagged "do not style" ([Styling FAQ](https://www.myindyx.com/blog/how-digital-styling-works-frequently-asked-questions)).

### Stylebook stays the private power tool, frozen since June 2025

Stylebook comes from husband-and-wife developers Jess and Bill Atkins and has been on sale since 2009. It costs **$4.99 once, with no in-app purchases and no account**. Closet data stays "on your device and in iCloud if enabled", and the developers say they cannot see it. Its App Store privacy label lists only anonymous product-interaction analytics, the cleanest in the category. The last release was version 10.1 on 15 June 2025 ([Stylebook FAQ](https://www.stylebookapp.com/faq.html); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id335709058)).

Capture offers the richest manual toolset in the set. Users can shoot, import from the library, paste several images at once, clip from a built-in web browser, pick from a basics catalogue, or generate an item with **Apple Intelligence text-to-image** ("red sweater, flat lay") on iOS 18.2 and later. Each item can hold multiple images, and instant AI background removal is backed by an eraser, a reverse eraser, tap-to-erase with a tolerance slider and a "Clean Up" pass that re-cuts old images ([AI background removal post](https://stylebookapptips.wordpress.com/2025/03/13/ai-background-removal/); [AI image generator post](https://stylebookapptips.wordpress.com/2025/03/13/al-image-generator/)).

Item notes hold more than 100 colour shades, brand and fabric autocomplete, and a price that converts automatically into cost per wear. Each item also carries a **status: clean, in the laundry, at the cleaners, lent out, wishlist or archived**. The closet is a two-level folder tree. **Outfit Shuffle** randomly deals ten outfits from three to eight chosen categories and is described as "the fashion lottery", not AI ([Features](https://www.stylebookapp.com/features.html); [Outfit Shuffle post](https://stylebookapptips.wordpress.com/2016/01/28/outfit-shuffle/)). The calendar keeps logging streaks and sends reminders, and packing lists propose items from the looks added to them and export as shareable infographics. Style Stats is the deepest analytics suite in the category. It includes Top-100 lists for most and least worn, best and worst cost per wear and highest purchase price, "Never Used in an Outfit" and "Not Logged on Calendar" lists, and a size tracker with per-brand exceptions ([Style Stats range post](https://stylebookapptips.wordpress.com/2018/01/12/style-stats-range/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id335709058)). What it lacks is equally clear: it runs on iPhone and iPad only, with no Android version, no social layer and no AI styling ([FAQ](https://www.stylebookapp.com/faq.html)).

### Cladwell rebuilt its capsule engine on LLMs in October 2026

Cladwell, founded in Cincinnati in 2016, was the original capsule-wardrobe app. Between 3 and 24 October 2026 it shipped **nine releases** that relaunched it as an LLM stylist. Onboarding now produces a "Style Map" from a style quiz and a colour season from three questions or a daylight photo. Most users never photograph anything: they pick look-alikes from a catalogue of about 15,000 items, paste product links, or bulk-upload photos for AI tagging ([cladwell.com FAQ](https://cladwell.com/faq)).

Capsules are the core abstraction. Daily outfits are generated within a selected capsule, each with a short stylist's note on why it works. A seasonal capsule of about **33 pieces** reports, for example, "22 of these 33 pieces are already in your closet". Each missing piece is scored by how many outfits it would unlock; one example says a dark-brown low heel "would add nine outfits". That gap becomes a shopping list of three to five real products within the user's budget ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1140550878); [cladwell.com](https://cladwell.com); [Should I buy this?](https://cladwell.com/faq/should-i-buy-this)).

A context-aware chat stylist has memory and can take actions on the user's behalf, and a ChatGPT connector exposes the closet to ChatGPT. The models are OpenAI's, plus others routed through OpenRouter to Azure and Vertex AI. Usage is metered through a **monthly AI allowance in which images cost more than chat** ([Cladwell privacy policy](https://cladwell.com/privacy-policy); [Cladwell in ChatGPT](https://cladwell.com/chatgpt)). Free users keep only the catalogue and wear tracking; "Your Stylist" costs $9.99 a month or $79.99 a year on the web ([cladwell.com/pricing](https://cladwell.com/pricing)). Cladwell's own report on 6,726 capsules found a **median capsule of 30 pieces** ([capsule report](https://cladwell.com/capsule-wardrobe-report)).

### Fits, Pureple, GetWardrobe and Essembl compete on feature density

**Fits** comes from a small German team (L. & J. Henne UG). It launched in May 2023 and shipped 25 releases between May and October 2026. Its free tier covers unlimited items, outfits and background removal, and AI auto-tags category, colour, season and any visible brand logo. Capture includes receipt forwarding and, since June 2026, a scan of the photo library that extracts clothes from outfit photos. Fits offers four outfit modes: a collage canvas, a swipeable "Dressing Room" mannequin, AI try-on at **one credit and up to about 40 seconds per render**, and an AI stylist chat with selectable personas. Its social layer adds OOTD stories, "This or That" polls, "Style @username" and counts of how many people have added a given piece. **Fits also runs an official MCP server** (`mcp.fits-app.com`) with OAuth, read tools such as `list_pieces` and `get_calendar`, write tools such as `save_outfit` and `remember`, and ChatGPT and Claude connectors, which makes it the most agent-ready app in the category. Pro ($9.99) adds unlimited AI chat, 30 credits a month and advanced stats ([Fits free vs paid](https://www.fits-app.com/knowledge/free-vs-paid-features); [Fits try-on guide](https://www.fits-app.com/knowledge/how-to-try-on-clothes-virtually-with-ai); [Fits MCP documentation](https://www.fits-app.com/mcp-documentation)).

**Pureple** (Iceclip LLC, 2013) was rebuilt around AI in 2025–26. It added "Style Me" suggestions, AI outfit feedback, and try-on "on you or a virtual model" using Google Gemini. OpenAI Vision tags each upload for category, colour, pattern, material, brand, season and occasion, and a Pro-only Whole Closet Scan detects several items in one closet photo (US rollout October 2026). Unlimited items and manual outfits are free with ads. The calendar, packing and stats sit behind Pro at **$6.99 a week, $14.99 a month or $89.99 a year**. Its App Store label says "Data Not Collected", yet its own policy describes Firebase analytics and transfers to OpenAI and Gemini ([Pureple privacy policy](https://pureple.com/Privacy.aspx); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id628106373)).

**GetWardrobe** (Outfit Makers LLC, since 2013, 41 languages) is the only app here with iOS, Android, macOS and web apps plus real-time sync. Outfit generation comes in three modes: an AI Outfit Generator whose free "Starter" model returns ten outfits per run (20- and 30-outfit runs cost credits), a free "AI Studio" with style, mood, occasion, weather and colour controls, and swipeable item pairings. Its weather planner suggests essentials such as sunscreen when UV is high. Family profiles and packing lists built from destination forecasts and bag weight are Premium. A $690-a-year Stylist Mode gives up to ten clients Premium. The free tier caps at 100 items, and full data export is offered ([getwardrobe.com](https://getwardrobe.com/); [pricing](https://getwardrobe.com/pricing/)).

**Essembl** (Essembl SA) redesigned in October 2026 into Today, Studio (with a "Rate me" score), Rate My Look and Shop tabs, and now detects clothes in the camera roll ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6479642952)). **Style DNA** (Barcelona) is the selfie-analysis specialist. One selfie yields a 12-season colour type, a body type and a Kibbe-style type in about 35 seconds. A GPT-4 chatbot with retrieval answers styling questions, five daily outfits are generated from the closet, and an in-store "item match" check scores a garment on colour, shape, print and fabric. An interested-party test by a colour-analysis vendor found AI tools got the broad season right only about half the time, and Style DNA reported **70K paying subscribers on 3.2M downloads** in mid-2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/21/style-dna-generative-ai-fashion-stylist-app); [Kettlewell](https://us.kettlewellcolours.com/blogs/the-color-blog/does-ai-color-analysis-work); [Mercado&Consumo](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/22/05/2026/inovacao/style-dna-um-personal-stylist-com-ia-que-cria-um-perfil-de-estilo-a-partir-de-uma-selfie/)).

### Combyne and ShopLook treat outfits as social content

Combyne is the volume giant, with 10M+ Android installs, and it plays like a Polyvore-style game. Users drag items from a huge shared library of user-uploaded and brand items onto a "Combyner" canvas with wallpapers and hairstyles, and every item credits its uploader. Engagement comes from user-created daily challenges, a For You, Following and Recent feed, follows, chat and groups. Visibility is monetised through Superlikes (10 for $0.99), a 2026 "Fire" currency earned through daily check-ins, and **Premium sold as "enhanced feed visibility"** at $2.99 a month or $19.99 a year. Its GDPR controller is now SIA URSTYLE in Riga, which revived the app after a 20-month update gap and added AI try-on, AI cut-outs, an ad marketplace and sponsored-item import ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id989727742); [Combyne imprint](https://www.combyne.com/imprint); [Combyne subscription](https://www.combyne.com/subscription)). ShopLook (San Francisco, 7,190 US ratings) is a self-described Polyvore successor with a 70M-image community library, collage effects, challenges and shoppable items ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1408832096)). Neither app logs wear or offers wardrobe stats, so both are weak closet planners.

### OpenWardrobe, Save Your Wardrobe and Smart Closet show the lifecycle and the decay

OpenWardrobe (Redwood City, 171 ratings) has the most complete lifecycle model in the set. Users keep several wardrobes, and **"Add to wardrobe" keeps an item in both without double-counting** while "move" transfers it, which cleanly models trips and capsules. The app values the closet through per-item resale value, value retention and a "True Cost per Wear" that includes unworn items. It shares closets through "Style Buddy" (read-only) and "Style Guru" (can edit) roles, adds a LolaAI stylist and a Chrome shopping copilot covering 700+ stores, and closes the loop with US alterations (free two-way shipping) and one-click Poshmark listing ([OpenWardrobe FAQ](https://www.openwardrobe.co/faq); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1549029468)).

Save Your Wardrobe's consumer app still books local repairs and, since mid-2026, issues Digital Service Passports. The company itself now sells aftersales software to LVMH, H&M France, Klarna and Kiabi ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1485757044); [Klarna release](https://www.saveyourwardrobe.com/en-gb/blog/save-your-wardrobe-powers-klarnas-care-and-repair-feature)). Smart Closet ($2.99) has a ShopStyle catalogue, a web clipper, a rule-based random-look generator and a deep stats list. It is now a cautionary tale: after a **35-month update gap**, October 2025 reviews averaged 1.21 on the App Store and 1.38 on Play amid login failures and wiped backups ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1198057728); [Smart Closet review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1198057728/sortby=mostrecent/json)).

### Shopping agents and try-on apps orbit the closet

Daydream, from former Stitch Fix COO Julie Bornstein, is a commission-only conversational fashion search engine over **roughly 3 million products from 10,000+ brands**, personalised through a "Style Passport". In 2026 it added "Suggest Less" negative feedback, quizzes and guest mode, which reads as a retrofit of recommender controls that pure chat under-collected. It also added iOS 27 Siri search, on-device product labelling, and a "Powered by Daydream" search product for brand websites. It has no closet, no try-on and **only 79 US ratings** ([TechCrunch, September 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/fashion-discovery-app-daydream-uses-apple-intelligence-to-help-you-shop-the-outfits-saved-in-your-camera-roll/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/daydream-ai-for-fashion/id6692634357)).

The other apps in this orbit each push one mechanic. **Gensmo** sells credits at **$0.50 each** for Snap-to-Shop, avatar try-on from one photo, couple try-on, walk-and-turn videos and gift planning ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gensmo-ai-stylist-try-on/id6636520663)). **Doji** trains a personal photoreal avatar with its own diffusion models from **six selfies and two full-body photos** (about 30 minutes at launch), and reviewers say it "makes everyone skinnier" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/15/doji-raises-14m-to-make-virtual-try-ons-fun-through-ai-avatars); [Doji review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=6737292650/sortby=mostrecent/json)). **Pronti** bolted a closet-aware chat stylist, "PAI", onto a 2021 closet app in February 2026 and charges for it with metered credits, though users still report mis-tagged items and nonsense combinations ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/outfit-maker-pronti-ai/id1560004583)). **Glance** (InMobi) generates free try-ons entirely on-device through Apple's Core AI, **Phia**, a deal-finder, added a closet and camera-roll import in October 2026, and **Dressly** has 32,661 US ratings built on weekly subscriptions of $7.99–$11.99 ([Glance](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/glance-my-personal-shopper/id6742974181); [Phia](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/phia-fashion-deals-rewards/id6739351340); [Dressly](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dressly-ai-outfit-maker/id6738174180)).

## One commodity pipeline powers most closed-source apps

None of the leaders publishes its architecture. The profiles below are reconstructed from store metadata, privacy policies, sub-processor lists, job posts, patents and public web bundles. Each claim is graded C (confirmed by first-party documents or production code), S (strongly indicated by two or more independent signals) or W (weakly indicated) ([Whering careers](https://whering.co.uk/careers); [Whering privacy](https://www.whering.co.uk/privacy); [Looko job post](https://www.wanted.co.kr/wd/391802); [altadaily.com bundle](https://www.altadaily.com/login); [Cladwell privacy](https://cladwell.com/privacy-policy); [Pureple privacy](http://www.pureple.com/Privacy.aspx); [Smart Closet AASA](https://smartcloset.me/.well-known/apple-app-site-association); [Daydream CEO on Kara Goldin](https://karagoldin.com/podcast/779); [NowSecure on Gensmo](https://www.nowsecure.com/?p=30563)).

Some of the clearest evidence leaked from production code. Alta's public web bundle contains server environment checks that name Supabase replicas, Redis and **Temporal** workflow endpoints. It also contains a model-tier list that includes `fast`, `fast_claude`, `reasoning_claude`, `fast_open_source`, `document_extraction` and `dress_codes`, plus per-category fine-tunes such as `ft_womens_dresses` and `ft_womens_shoes`. Weather-feel thresholds sit next to it (86°F or above is "scalding") ([altadaily.com/login](https://www.altadaily.com/login)). That matches the founder's description of "a generative multimodal model composed of 12+ submodels" ([Alta/Cannes release](https://www.altadaily.com/blog/cannes)). Whering's web app calls a REST API at `api.whering.co.uk`; its marketplace "similar items" search service is named `dionne` and a newsletter service `cher`, both after *Clueless* characters ([app.whering.co.uk](https://app.whering.co.uk/login)). An Acloset hiring post says the company is turning the app into **"an MCP-based next-generation digital closet platform"** that AI agents can use directly ([Wanted](https://www.wanted.co.kr/wd/391802)).

Each feature follows a recognisable pattern.

**Cut-outs** come from a paid API (Whering uses remove.bg), from on-device OS segmentation (likely at Stylebook, whose iOS 17 floor and near-empty privacy label fit Apple Vision subject lifting, though this is weakly indicated) or, increasingly, from generative clean-up into catalogue-style images, as in Alta's Prettify, Acloset's Beautify Pro, the Enhance features at Indyx and Whering, and Cladwell's "Improve photo". Acloset's patents describe the classical route instead. They cover extracting garments from a photo that includes the wearer's face while logging wear dates and frequency, correcting garment shape from per-type keypoints, and estimating garment size by measuring "folding amount" ([US20230140225A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230140225A1/en); [KR102824768B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/KR102824768B1/en); [KR20250087080A](https://patents.google.com/patent/KR20250087080A/en)).

**Tagging** is either bought or trained. Whering uses Pixyle, and Pureple sends every upload to OpenAI's Vision API ([Pureple privacy](http://www.pureple.com/Privacy.aspx)). Acloset trains its own models on a dataset built from users' clothing uploads, and Alta's `ft_womens_*` tiers suggest it does too ([Acloset 2022 press release](https://business.minstercommunitypost.com/minstercommunitypost/article/getnews-2022-11-24-acloset-is-taking-hyper-personalization-to-the-fashion-industry-through-the-ai-powered-digital-closet-platform)).

**Imports** rely on standard mechanisms: Gmail access under Google's Limited Use policy (Acloset, Indyx), personal forwarding addresses (Acloset, Indyx, Fits), logging in to a store's order history inside an in-app browser (Acloset), Chrome extensions (Whering, Acloset, OpenWardrobe) and ChatGPT reading receipts or links (Cladwell).

**Outfit generation** is overwhelmingly an LLM prompted with a structured summary of the closet plus context. Pureple sends "wardrobe item descriptions, location, weather data, and language preference", and its chat receives the 20 most recent messages ([Pureple privacy](http://www.pureple.com/Privacy.aspx)). Alta classifies calendar events with TypeSafe before Vertex AI generates outfits. Cladwell pulls weather from Apple WeatherKit and Open-Meteo, and its release notes say starter outfits appear first while requested looks "appear as they're styled", which suggests cheap precomputed outfits backed by on-demand generation ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1140550878)). Long-running generations sit on durable job and workflow systems: Temporal at Alta, a "gem-workflow" service at Gensmo and BullMQ at Whering.

**Shopping and similarity search** run on vectors. Acloset uses the Qdrant vector database. Daydream's pending patent describes a pipeline in which an LLM breaks a request such as "like this but longer" into modifications, an embedding model predicts where the target product should sit, and a vector search with structured filters retrieves matches ([US20260099870A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20260099870A1/en)).

**Try-on** is mostly bought from Google. Pureple and Style DNA both name Gemini. Doppl ran Google Shopping's custom fashion model, and its launch post was co-written by a co-author of Google's **TryOnDiffusion** (CVPR 2023). Google now sells the capability as Vertex AI `virtual-try-on-001`, generally available since 20 January 2026, with up to four outputs per call ([Google blog, Doppl](https://blog.google/technology/google-labs/doppl/); [TryOnDiffusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08276); [Vertex AI docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/models/imagen/virtual-try-on-001)). Google's December 2025 selfie flow uses Nano Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) to synthesise a full-body version of the user from a headshot and their usual clothing size ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/11/googles-ai-try-on-feature-for-clothes-now-works-with-just-a-selfie/)).

**Agent access** is the newest layer: Fits' MCP server, Cladwell's ChatGPT app, Acloset's planned MCP platform, and Siri entry points at Daydream and Glance.

## Open-source code shows how to build it, and where the licence traps sit

None of the leading apps is open source, but six open-source wardrobe apps and the reference ML implementations show what each feature takes to build. The code reviewed here is pinned to commits read on 25 October 2026.

**wardrowbe** (MIT licence, 803 stars, last commit 24 October 2026) is by far the most production-like. It runs on Next.js, FastAPI, Postgres, Redis and arq background workers, ships Kubernetes manifests and has about 140 test files.

Its tagger sends each photo to any OpenAI-compatible vision model with a JSON-only prompt. The prompt injects closed vocabularies (32 types, 20 colours, 15 materials, six formality levels) from a single JSON file shared by backend and frontend. The tagger then scores per-field confidence from **token log-probabilities**, weighted 0.30 for type and 0.25 for colour, and records whether each tag came from the AI or the user ([clothing_analysis prompt](https://github.com/Anyesh/wardrowbe/blob/0bbca3a4d92b9d3cec64a5a09ffdacce41f04d04/backend/app/prompts/clothing_analysis.txt); [ai_service.py](https://github.com/Anyesh/wardrowbe/blob/0bbca3a4d92b9d3cec64a5a09ffdacce41f04d04/backend/app/services/ai_service.py#L129-L254)).

Its recommender is the best public template for the category, and it works in four stages. First, a deterministic score per item multiplies weather, formality (1.0 inside the occasion's allowed set, 0.5 one step away, 0.15 otherwise), season, recency (0.1 if worn today, recovering linearly), preference and a 1.15 usage boost for neglected items, and keeps the top 70. Second, those 70 items go to an LLM as **integer-numbered lines**, with a prompt that encodes colour harmony ("Limit to 3 distinct colors maximum"), proportion and layering and asks for three outfits. Third, the server maps the numbers back to item IDs, drops unknown ones, re-inserts mandatory pieces, removes duplicate body slots, drops tights on hot days and fills any missing body region deterministically. Fourth, each outfit earns a learning signal (accepted +0.3, rejected −0.5, worn +0.3, "wore something else instead" −0.4), and item-pair compatibility and user profiles are recomputed hourly. Weather uses the minimum and maximum forecast over the remaining "wearing window" until 9pm, flips seasons for the southern hemisphere, and lets users set their own cold and hot thresholds ([item_scorer.py](https://github.com/Anyesh/wardrowbe/blob/0bbca3a4d92b9d3cec64a5a09ffdacce41f04d04/backend/app/services/item_scorer.py#L206-L528); [recommendation_service.py](https://github.com/Anyesh/wardrowbe/blob/0bbca3a4d92b9d3cec64a5a09ffdacce41f04d04/backend/app/services/recommendation_service.py#L138-L198); [learning_service.py](https://github.com/Anyesh/wardrowbe/blob/0bbca3a4d92b9d3cec64a5a09ffdacce41f04d04/backend/app/services/learning_service.py#L420-L672); [weather_service.py](https://github.com/Anyesh/wardrowbe/blob/0bbca3a4d92b9d3cec64a5a09ffdacce41f04d04/backend/app/services/weather_service.py#L362-L389)).

The other five repositories are smaller, but each teaches one thing.

The ML components behind each feature, with the commercially usable choices and the licence traps, are summarised below ([rembg README](https://github.com/danielgatis/rembg/blob/c5b48b0417a0254df7d461efa14573597f369a83/README.md#L533); [BiRefNet](https://github.com/ZhengPeng7/BiRefNet); [ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/subject-segmentation); [SCHP](https://github.com/GoGoDuck912/Self-Correction-Human-Parsing); [SegFormer clothes](https://huggingface.co/mattmdjaga/segformer_b2_clothes); [Marqo-FashionSigLIP](https://huggingface.co/Marqo/marqo-fashionSigLIP); [outfit-transformer](https://github.com/owj0421/outfit-transformer/blob/eef4be55dbdc2f86f47e3a5d9d4b85c2f17de08b/readme.md#L25-L32); [FASHN VTON v1.5](https://github.com/fashn-AI/fashn-vton-1.5); [CatVTON](https://github.com/Zheng-Chong/CatVTON); [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)).

|---|---|---|---|---|

Three engineering lessons follow from this code.

**Cut-outs.** The cheap production pattern is an instant on-device preview, asynchronous server refinement with BiRefNet, always keeping the original photo, and an erase/restore brush for failures. Paid APIs cost from **$0.02 an image at Photoroom** to roughly $0.20 at remove.bg, against near zero on-device or self-hosted ([Photoroom pricing](https://www.photoroom.com/api/pricing)).

**Outfit recommendation.** Research far outscores what shipping apps use. Outfit-compatibility models have improved from 2017's sequence models through type-aware embeddings (AUC 0.86) to set transformers (AUC 0.93–0.95), yet every shipping open-source app still uses weighted rules plus an LLM. A production design would put hard constraints (slots, weather, formality, laundry) in front of a learned compatibility scorer running on cached FashionSigLIP embeddings. It would then re-rank per user from accept, reject and wear signals, and use the LLM only for explanation and natural-language requests.

**Licences.** Diligence must cover code, weights *and* training data. MIT-licensed code does not make a model commercially safe: even MIT-coded Leffa was trained on VITON-HD, whose licence says "research purposes only" ([VITON-HD](https://github.com/shadow2496/VITON-HD); [Leffa](https://github.com/franciszzj/Leffa)).

## Closet subscriptions stall while brand and commerce money flows

Consumer pricing falls into five archetypes: free with AI credits, an uncapped free tier with a paid membership, item-capped freemium, hard paywalls with weekly plans, and paid-upfront apps. The VC-backed newcomers, Alta and Daydream, charge consumers nothing at all ([Whering](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1519461680), [Acloset](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1542311809), [Indyx](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1599179405), [Pureple](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id628106373), [Style DNA](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1358319821), [Dressly](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6738174180), [Fits](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6447482321) and [OpenWardrobe](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1549029468) App Store listings; [Cladwell pricing](https://cladwell.com/pricing); [GetWardrobe pricing](https://getwardrobe.com/pricing/)).

Apple's in-app purchase lists also hint at price testing. Cladwell lists four or more annual prices, Pureple five "Pureple Subscription" SKUs and Style DNA three monthly prices, which points to ongoing paywall experiments and grandfathered plans.

The pricing unit is moving from storage to AI usage. Indyx's founder made the case against item caps directly: "the average wardrobe size is about 175 items… even if the paywall kicks in after your 100th item… why start?" ([Indyx blog](https://www.myindyx.com/blog/why-we-are-launching-insider)). Newer entrants advertise unlimited items and charge for generation instead, because generation has a real marginal cost. A try-on costs about **$0.039 on Gemini 2.5 Flash Image** and $0.049–$0.075 on FASHN's API, while retail credits sell for roughly $0.07 to $0.50 ([FASHN API pricing](https://help.fashn.ai/plans-and-pricing/api-pricing); [Future AGI price tracker](https://futureagi.com/llm-cost-calculator/google/gemini-2-5-flash-image); [Gensmo](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6636520663)).

Whering's cheapest pack, the help centre's 100 credits for £6.99, works out to about $0.09 a credit, or roughly $0.077 after Apple's 15% small-business commission. That is about FASHN's on-demand price, and Whering also charges for every regeneration ([Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)). Healthy margins therefore need a model costing $0.04 or less, or prices two to three times the cost of generation. Four ways of capping AI costs now coexist: monthly allowances (Cladwell, and Fits' 30 credits a month), fair-use clauses (Indyx's "unlimited" enhancements), daily free quotas with paid upgrades (Acloset's Basic and Pro try-on), and pure pay-per-use (Whering, Gensmo).

No pure closet subscription has reached venture scale. Cladwell's subscription revenue peaked at "nearly $1M", and when it sold in August 2019 it had about **$330K ARR and nearly 10,000 paid subscribers**, after its founders "hit a growth ceiling" ([TheyGotAcquired](https://theygotacquired.com/saas/cladwell-acquired/)). Style DNA's 70K payers on 3.2M downloads is a download-to-paid rate of about 2.2%. At its $29.99–$39.99 annual prices that would imply roughly $2–3M in gross annual bookings, though that figure is an estimate, not a reported number ([Mercado&Consumo](https://mercadoeconsumo.com.br/22/05/2026/inovacao/style-dna-um-personal-stylist-com-ia-que-cria-um-perfil-de-estilo-a-partir-de-uma-selfie/)). Acloset is the only leader claiming break-even, with revenue growing 70–120% a year, and it gets there with caps, ads and a virtual currency ([Wanted](https://www.wanted.co.kr/wd/391802)).

Public filings show how thin the classic closet businesses run. Whering held **£11,007 in cash** on 30 June 2025, against net liabilities of £347K, £236K owed in taxes and social security and £203K in director loans, with implied losses of about £1M a year over FY2023–FY2025. Its 14 October 2026 share register still showed the founder holding about 74.5% of ordinary shares, with no eBay or Google entity yet listed ([Whering FY2025 accounts](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/12660509/filing-history/MzUxMzY4MTU1MmFkaXF6a2N4/document?format=xhtml&download=1); [confirmation statement](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/12660509/filing-history/MzU1MTIxNjQ0M2FkaXF6a2N4/document?format=pdf&download=0)). Save Your Wardrobe lost about £1.75M in FY2025 and carries £5.57M of convertible notes due to convert at its Series A ([SYW FY2025 accounts](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/10831653/filing-history/MzUyMzcxMzgwMGFkaXF6a2N4/document?format=xhtml&download=1)). By contrast, Daydream's $50M seed is confirmed by two SEC Form D filings totalling **$49.66M** ([Form D](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2008800/000200880024000003/0002008800-24-000003-index.htm)).

Claimed user counts are cumulative sign-ups, not active users. AppGoblin's modelled figures, a low-confidence third-party estimate, put Whering's monthly actives at about 232K on Android and 86K on iOS against its 10M+ claim. They put Acloset's at about 233K on Android against 8–9M claimed, and Alta's at about 157K on iOS ([AppGoblin, Whering](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.whering.app); [AppGoblin, Alta](https://appgoblin.info/apps/6481705400)).

The money that could reach venture scale is being pursued outside consumer subscriptions. Alta earns affiliate cuts through Saks, Bloomingdale's, Nordstrom, Rakuten and Sovrn across roughly 4,000 brands, and increasingly presents itself as **B2B AI infrastructure**: "Our goal is to be the best possible AI provider for this space" ([WWD](https://wwd.com/fashion-news/fashion-features/cfda-partnership-alta-personal-styling-app-ai-1237089028/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/alta-ai-fashion-app-series-a-20-million-forerunner-rent-the-runway/)). Daydream takes commission only, says it is "not looking to generate any ad-based revenue", and adds search for brand websites ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/20/former-stitch-fix-coo-julie-bornstein-secures-50m-to-build-a-new-age-e-commerce-search-engine)). Save Your Wardrobe sells aftersales software and bought the French repair platform Les Raccommodeurs ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/save-your-wardrobe-acquires-french-aftersales-platform-les-raccommodeurs/2025093084172)), and Whering frames its eBay-backed future around resale and becoming "a definitive data source" for circular retail ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/whering-secures-funding-from-ebay-ventures-and-google-ai-futures/2026070773352)). Indyx rejects affiliate links as "disingenuous" and sells human services instead ([Indyx blog](https://www.myindyx.com/blog/why-we-are-launching-insider)). Ads remain the older mass-market model: Acloset, Combyne and Pureple carry "Contains ads" on Play, and AppGoblin models ads at about two-thirds of Combyne's and Pureple's Android revenue ([AppGoblin, Combyne](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.combyne.app)).

Growth is overwhelmingly organic and press-led. The *Clueless* closet is the category's shared pitch. Whering cites Vogue and appeared on BBC's *Dragons' Den*, Alta has TIME and Vogue mentions plus celebrity angels, and Pureple's best-known moment was Safiya Nygaard's video, claimed at 11M+ views ([pureple.com](https://www.pureple.com)). On TikTok, Whering leads on volume and Alta on engagement per video. The best-funded shopping agents have almost no TikTok following; they grow through press and platform deals instead ([TikTok @whering](https://www.tiktok.com/@whering); [TikTok @altadaily](https://www.tiktok.com/@altadaily); [TikTok @daydreamshopping](https://www.tiktok.com/@daydreamshopping)).

## Conclusion

The "best" app depends on the job. Whering is the pick for free, social use; Alta for generative styling and avatars; Acloset for automated capture, if you will pay past 100 items; Indyx for analytics and human help; and Stylebook for privacy at a one-time price. Beneath that variety, the category's real dividing line is **who absorbs the cost of cataloguing, and whether the bill arrives before or after that effort**. The apps users love keep the closet free or charge once up front. They charge only for things with a visible marginal cost, such as generation or a human stylist. The apps users punish monetise the sunk effort itself. That reframes the AI arms race, because generation is now cheap and rented. Even the model behind Acloset's Beautify Pro, Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, appears on Google's pricing page with a 2 October 2026 shutdown date ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). And Google Photos and ChatGPT now give away closets built from photo libraries and screenshot try-on. What independents can actually defend is a clean, user-owned record of what someone owns and wears, the community built on top of it, and what that record is worth to brands.

The evidence also shows where the open space is. No app yet combines Acloset-grade capture without photography; Stylebook-grade privacy, export and laundry status; a learned compatibility model in place of rules plus an LLM; calendar-to-occasion styling, which is absent from all open-source code; try-on conditioned on body measurements, which users keep asking for; and billing users trust. The next year will probably bring more B2B pivots among the try-on apps that burn money on credits, more agent access through MCP and OS assistants, and closet data increasingly treated as a resale and commerce asset.
